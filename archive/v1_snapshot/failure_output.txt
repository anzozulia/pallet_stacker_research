### F1 Pareto continuum

  Got: 5 pallets, 60.4% util, 0 unpacked
  Theoretical LB: 4 pallets (gap: +1)
  Verdict: FAILURE — uses 1 more pallet(s) than necessary.
  Better approach: An oracle that runs an exact MIP solver (do Nascimento et al. 2021) would close this gap; for heuristics, GRASP + maximal-space (Parreño et al. 2008) typically gains 5–10 pp utilisation.
  Literature reference: Parreño et al. (2008); do Nascimento et al. (2021)

### F2 large block of identicals
  Description: 24 identical small boxes (20×20×30) + 6 medium (60×50×70). The 24 identicals SHOULD be packed as a single block; our one-at-a-time placer disperses them and fragments the pallet.
  Got: 2 pallets, 64.5% util, 0 unpacked
  Theoretical LB: 2 pallets (gap: +0)
  Verdict: PASS — algorithm matches lower bound.
  Better approach: Eley (2002) block-building: identify identical SKUs, pack as rectangular blocks of (n_x × n_y × n_z) units, then arrange the blocks. A 6×4×1 block of the small boxes occupies 120×80×30 — leaving a clean 120×100×70 space for the medium boxes.
  Literature reference: Eley (2002); Bortfeldt (2000)

### F3 interlock big+small

  Theoretical LB: 1 pallets (gap: +1)
  Verdict: FAILURE — uses 1 more pallet(s) than necessary.
  Better approach: Layer-/wall-building heuristics (Bischoff & Ratcliff 1995; George & Robinson 1980) construct each layer with a mix of compatible SKUs, exactly the structure this case demands.
  Literature reference: George & Robinson (1980); Bischoff & Ratcliff (1995)

### F4 height pairing
  Description: 8 boxes that pair into 4 columns of pallet-height 100mm each (60+40 and 70+30). Optimal: 1 pallet, 100% util. The heuristic picks heaviest-first (X70 weighs 4kg, then T60 at 3kg) and stacks short-on-tall, wasting headroom.
  Got: 1 pallets, 100.0% util, 0 unpacked
  Theoretical LB: 1 pallets (gap: +0)
  Verdict: PASS — algorithm matches lower bound.
  Better approach: Pre-process: pair items whose heights sum to pallet H (column construction, Bortfeldt 2000). Or post-process: identify two short columns that would consolidate into one full column.
  Literature reference: Bortfeldt (2000) column construction

### F5 wrong-rotation cascade

  Literature reference: Bortfeldt & Gehring (2001); SKU-consistent rotation

### F8 strongly heterogeneous

### F10 overhang would help (but isn't used)
  Description: 4 boxes 70×60×100 (matching pallet height, this-side-up). WITHOUT overhang: 1 fits per pallet → 4 pallets needed. WITH 20 mm overhang permitted: 2 fit per pallet (one box has right edge at 70, second at 140 = 20 mm overhang) → 2 pallets. Our heuristic never proposes overhanging placements, so even with permission it gets the worse answer.
  Got: 1 pallets, 140.0% util, 0 unpacked
  Theoretical LB: 2 pallets (gap: +-1)
  Verdict: ?
  Better approach: Add overhang-aware extreme points: when generating new EPs after a placement, also propose positions where the next box could occupy the up-to-max_overhang strip beyond the pallet's right/back edges. Maximal-space representations (Parreño et al. 2008) naturally model these as 'extended' empty spaces.
  Literature reference: Parreño et al. (2008) maximal-space + overhang EPs

### F11 greedy leaves an item unpacked
  Description: 6 medium boxes + 1 tall narrow box. The narrow box only fits if a corner is left free; the heuristic's heaviest-first sort fills those corners with medium boxes. Tests whether the algorithm wrongly leaves the narrow box unpacked, or finds a configuration.
  Got: 1 pallets, 27.3% util, 0 unpacked
  Theoretical LB: 1 pallets (gap: +0)
  Verdict: PASS — algorithm matches lower bound.
  Better approach: GRASP (Greedy Randomized Adaptive Search Procedure) restarts from many random orderings; with enough restarts a feasible configuration emerges. Repair heuristics (Tabu Search ejection chains) can also rescue an unpacked item by displacing items.
  Literature reference: Parreño et al. (2010) GRASP; Crainic et al. (2009) TS²PACK

### F12 strongly heterogeneous at scale

  Got: 3 pallets, 65.4% util, 0 unpacked
  Theoretical LB: 2 pallets (gap: +1)
  Verdict: FAILURE — uses 1 more pallet(s) than necessary.
  Better approach: True BRKGA with population recombination (Gonçalves & Resende 2013) typically beats single-decoder heuristics by 2-3 pp utilisation on these instances. For under 30 boxes per pallet, exact MIP polish (do Nascimento et al. 2021) can close further.
  Literature reference: Gonçalves & Resende (2013); do Nascimento et al. (2021)

### F13 pure layered cargo
  Description: 22 boxes, all H=50, pallet H=100 = exactly 2 layers. Mixed footprints. A layer-building heuristic would tile each layer independently. Our column-oriented placer treats it as a 3D problem and may waste capacity.
  Got: 2 pallets, 70.0% util, 0 unpacked
  Theoretical LB: 2 pallets (gap: +0)
  Verdict: PASS — algorithm matches lower bound.
  Better approach: When all (or most) boxes share a height, the problem reduces to 2D bin packing per layer. George & Robinson (1980) wall-building and Bischoff-Ratcliff (1995) layer-building exploit this directly; running a 2D-MaxRects (`rectpack`) per layer can be far better than 3D EP.
  Literature reference: George & Robinson (1980); Burke, Kendall & Whitwell (2004)
